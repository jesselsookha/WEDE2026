# Website Strategy and Measurement

By now, you understand **why websites matter** and **what makes them effective**. In this final part of the unit, we connect websites to the **broader strategic goals** of organizations — how they serve business objectives, how we measure their success, and how we make informed decisions based on real user behaviour. Even a beautiful, well-crafted website cannot be considered successful unless it actively supports the organization's mission, whether that mission involves generating revenue, informing the public, raising donations, or building trust and credibility.

---

## Ⅰ. Aligning Websites with Organizational Goals

Different organizations pursue different goals, and their websites must reflect those priorities accordingly. A website is not a generic tool; it is a strategic asset that should be tailored to the specific needs and objectives of its owner.

### A. Business Versus Non-Profit Goals

The table below illustrates how website goals vary across different types of organizations.

| Type of Organization | Website Goal Examples |
|----------------------|-----------------------|
| E-commerce | Drive sales, build trust, reduce cart abandonment |
| Service Business | Generate leads, bookings, or enquiries |
| Non-Profit | Increase donations, recruit volunteers, raise awareness |
| Educational | Share knowledge, register students, provide learning resources |

The fundamental question that every organization must ask is this: What does the website need to accomplish for the organization to succeed? The answer to this question shapes every subsequent decision about content, design, and functionality.

### B. Design That Serves Strategy

Different strategic goals lead to different design choices. A restaurant website, for example, might need clear menu pages, an integrated location map with directions, and an online booking or ordering system that allows customers to reserve tables or place takeaway orders with ease. A non-profit organization, by contrast, might focus on emotional storytelling through powerful imagery and narratives, prominent and repeated "Donate Now" buttons placed strategically throughout the site, and transparency elements such as financial reports, impact metrics, and testimonials from beneficiaries.

Design is not decoration; it is direction. Every element on a website should serve a purpose and support the organization's overarching strategy. When design choices are made without strategic clarity, the website risks becoming a collection of attractive but ultimately ineffective features.

## Ⅱ. Measuring Website Success

How do we determine whether a website is actually working? Intuition and subjective impressions are unreliable guides. Instead, we use **Key Performance Indicators**, commonly known as KPIs. These are measurable values that provide objective evidence of whether we are achieving our goals.

### A. Examples of Common KPIs

The table below outlines some of the most frequently used website KPIs and what they reveal about performance.

| KPI | What It Tells You |
|-----|-------------------|
| New Visitors | Whether your reach is growing and attracting fresh audiences |
| Bounce Rate | Whether visitors are leaving too quickly without engaging |
| Time on Page | Whether users are genuinely engaged with the content |
| Form Submissions | Whether people are taking action, such as signing up or making enquiries |
| Conversion Rate | Whether visits are turning into tangible results, such as sales or sign-ups |
| Click-Through Rate | Whether calls to action and links are capturing engagement |

Think of KPIs as a fitness tracker for your website. They reveal progress, highlight problems, and uncover opportunities for improvement. Without them, you are navigating blindly.

### B. SMART KPIs

Effective KPIs follow the **SMART** principle, a framework that ensures goals are well-defined and actionable.

A KPI should be **Specific**: clear and focused on a single objective. It should be **Measurable**: trackable using available data and analytics tools. It should be **Achievable**: realistic given available resources, time, and capabilities. It should be **Relevant**: directly tied to organizational goals and priorities. Finally, it should be **Time-bound**: having a defined deadline or regular review schedule.

An example of a SMART KPI might be: "Increase newsletter sign-ups by twenty per cent within three months." This goal is specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound, making it a useful target for guiding effort and evaluating success.

## Ⅲ. The HITS Framework

A helpful and memorable way to plan and review website effectiveness is the **HITS** framework. HITS stands for *How Idiots Track Success* — a deliberately informal acronym designed to make the process easy to recall and apply.

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| H (Human/Audience) | Who is the website for? What are their needs, preferences, and online habits? |
| I (Intent/Organization) | What does the organization need to achieve through the site? |
| T (Tools/Content) | What features, content, and functions will help achieve those goals? |
| S (Success Indicators) | How will we measure whether the site is fulfilling its purpose? |

HITS helps align strategy with structure by emphasizing a logical sequence. Always start with users and purpose before jumping into design or development. When you understand who you are serving and what you need to accomplish, the appropriate tools and success metrics become far clearer.

## Ⅳ. Calculating Return on Investment

### A. What Is Return on Investment?

Return on Investment, commonly abbreviated as ROI, is a financial metric that evaluates the efficiency of an investment. The formula is straightforward:

**ROI = (Return – Cost) / Cost**

This calculation tells you whether the time and money spent on a website are delivering value. A higher ROI indicates that the website is generating significant returns relative to its cost. A low or negative ROI suggests that the investment is not paying off and may require reconsideration.

### B. Return on Investment Isn't Always About Money

It is important to recognize that not every website has purely financial goals. Blogs, personal portfolios, artistic showcases, and non-profit sites often measure success in non-monetary terms. Other forms of return might include increased visibility and brand awareness, improved user engagement and loyalty, higher sign-up or download numbers, increased volunteer recruitment, or enhanced brand reputation and trust.

Success can be measured in trust, influence, and community impact, not just in cash.

### C. Practical Example

The table below illustrates how different types of websites might evaluate their ROI.

| Website Type | Potential Investment | Returns Measured By |
|--------------|----------------------|---------------------|
| E-commerce | R30,000 setup plus R2,000 per month | Sales revenue, average order value, cart abandonment rate |
| Tutorial Blog | R1,000 setup plus ongoing time investment | Traffic growth, time spent on page, backlinks from other sites |
| Non-Profit | R15,000 setup | Donations received, volunteer sign-ups, audience reach |
| Bed and Breakfast | R8,000 setup | Booking requests, map clicks, contact form submissions |

## Ⅴ. Fundamentals Done Right

Before investing in fancy features or complex functionality, a successful website needs solid foundations. The basics are often the most important elements, and neglecting them undermines everything else.

### Checklist of Essentials

- Clear and consistent navigation that works across all pages
- Fast loading pages that respect users' time
- Working internal and external links with no broken pathways
- Mobile-friendly design that adapts to all screen sizes
- Up-to-date and accurate content that reflects current information
- Accessible layout with sufficient contrast, alt text, and readable fonts
- Visible and usable contact information, including name, phone number, and email address
- A Google Maps embed where physical location is relevant to the business

Build the house before decorating it. A website with strong fundamentals will always outperform one with impressive visuals but weak foundations.

## Ⅵ. The Technology Behind It All

Every website is built using three core technologies, each serving a distinct role in the final product.

| Technology | Role |
|------------|------|
| HTML | Structure — headings, paragraphs, images, links, and content organization |
| CSS | Design — colours, layouts, fonts, and visual styling |
| JavaScript | Behaviour — animations, form validation, interactivity, and dynamic content |

A useful analogy is to think of HTML as the bricks and walls that form the structure of a building. CSS is the paint, wallpaper, and decorative finishes that make it visually appealing. JavaScript is the electricity and moving parts — the functionality that makes the space usable and responsive to its occupants.

We will explore these technologies in detail during the practical sessions, but it helps to understand how they work together to create a complete web experience.

## Ⅶ. Assets and Legal Considerations

### A. Using Assets

When building a website, you will inevitably need images, text, icons, and other media. It is essential to use only licensed or free-to-use resources and to credit creators when required by the license terms. Copying large blocks of text from other websites without permission is not only unethical but also potentially illegal. Respecting intellectual property rights is a fundamental responsibility of any web developer.

Reliable sources for free, high-quality assets include Unsplash and Pexels for photography, Font Awesome for icons, and Google Fonts for typography. These resources provide professional-grade materials without the legal and financial risks associated with unlicensed content.

### B. Optimizing Assets

Large image files are one of the primary causes of slow websites. Even beautiful visuals become a liability if they degrade performance. Use tools to reduce file size without sacrificing quality. TinyPNG, Adobe Express, and Squoosh are all effective options for compressing images. It is also wise to resize images for their actual use rather than uploading oversized files. There is no benefit to displaying a five-thousand-pixel-wide image in a three-hundred-pixel space; it only slows down the site and wastes bandwidth.
